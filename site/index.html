The Trust-but-Verify Manual for What AI Tells You

A friendly, step-by-step guide to checking AI-generated claims before you put your name on them.

By Dylan Asdale · The Massive AI Manual · Asdale Dynamics, LLC

Who this is for

If you write anything — essays, blog posts, newsletters, book chapters, school papers, LinkedIn posts — and you use AI to help, this manual is for you.

Here's the honest truth: AI tools are fantastic drafting partners and terrible fact-checkers. They write with the confidence of a professor and the memory of a guy who skimmed the book once in 2022. Sometimes that confidence is backed by real knowledge. Sometimes it's a beautiful, fluent, completely invented story.

Good writers who use AI well are not the ones who trust AI the most. They're the ones who verify the fastest. This manual teaches you how to do exactly that, in plain English, without feeling stupid about it.

The one-sentence version

Every fact in your draft is either (a) something you know first-hand, (b) something you have personally re-checked against a real source, or (c) something you hedge honestly — and that's not a weakness, that's professionalism.

The 60-second version (for when you're in a hurry)

  1. Spot the claims. Read your AI-assisted draft and mark every sentence that states a fact: numbers, dates, quotes, names, "studies show," "experts say."
  2. Chase each claim to a real source. Not the AI's word for it — an actual page, report, book, or dataset you opened yourself.
  3. Hedge what you can't verify. Use honest phrasing (Chapter 3) instead of deleting the idea.
  4. Log your work. Keep a claim ledger so future-you knows what was checked and when.

How to use this manual

  • If you're about to publish something, jump straight to Chapter 2 and run the workflow end to end. Then use Chapter 4 worksheets as you go.
  • If you're learning how this all works, read Chapters 1–3 in order. They build on each other.
  • If you teach or lead a team, Chapters 2 and 4 work as-is for workshops and onboarding.
